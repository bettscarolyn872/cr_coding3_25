# Mini Project Planning/Development Session

## Mini Project Guidance
Your mini project should demonstrate how computer vision enables new robotic behaviors. Rather than prescribing specific projects, consider these guiding questions:

### Start with a Problem or Interaction
- What human-robot interaction interests you?
- What task could a robot do better with vision?
- What creative expression becomes possible when a robot can "see"?

### Think About Context
- Social robots: How might a robot respond to human emotions or gestures?
- Creative robots: How could vision enable artistic collaboration?
- Helper robots: What everyday tasks need visual understanding?
- Playful robots: How could vision make robots more engaging?

### Consider Your Technical Approach
- Which detection type suits your concept? (face, pose, object, etc.)
- Does your robot need to track movement or recognize states?
- Is real-time response important for your application?

### Robotics Relevance (No Hardware Required)

Your project should explain how your vision system would enable robotic behavior:
- Movement responses (even if just simulated)
- Decision-making based on visual input
- Interaction patterns that change with what's detected

### What We're Looking For
- Originality: Your unique take on human-robot interaction
- Understanding: Clear grasp of how ML enables your concept
- Iteration: Evidence of testing and refining your approach
- Reflection: Thoughtful analysis of successes and challenges

### Avoid:
- Generic implementations without creative application
- Projects that could work without vision (e.g., random behaviors)
- Direct copies of tutorial projects without substantial modification

**The best projects often come from personal interests or observations about where robots could be more intelligent or responsive through vision.**

Spend 10 minutes considering how you want to share your idea or the progress of your Mini project with your peer(s). Use the prompt questions below if you like.

When you share your idea or progress, consider what you would like feedback on from your peer(s). You may directly ask for particular feedback if you like.

## Planning Prompt Questions:
- What are your main ideas?  
- How would you like to approach it?  
- What resources do you need to produce the project?  
- Why do you want to work on it? (if applicable)  
- How feasible is it to work on the project?  
- Potentially, may you face any barriers to working on the project?  
- What is your timeline to develop the project?

## Building on Progress Prompt Questions:
- How has the progress been so far?  
- What has gone well?  
- Did you have to make any changes to your plan?  
- Are you facing any problem you would like to solve - whether technical or creative (or both)?  
- What are the resources that you have that you can use to progress with the development?  
- How can you still improve your project?  
- Within the timeframe you have, how much do you think you can progress?

## Other Things You Could Consider for Creative Applications (Where Relevant):
- How is the "creativity" of the project framed?  
  *(For reference, the section below provides a few quotations concerning approaches of creative robotics by Samani, 2023)*  
- Is your project providing a human-computer/robot interaction? If so, how does the functionality go in synergy with the creative side?  
- Is your project providing any human-robot collaboration? If so, how does the functionality contribute to the collaborative creative process? Is there still a way to improve the collaborative “workflow” through tweaking the code and/or system?


## Quotations Concerning Approaches of Creative Robotics  
From Samani, H. (2023) *Creative Robotics*.

Creative robotics in art encompasses a diverse range of mediums and practices that explore the fusion of technology, robotics, and artistic expression. Within this chapter, we explore some of the most prominent artistic applications of creative robotics. Interactive installations invite viewers to engage in participatory and immersive experiences, blurring the boundaries between art and audience.

Kinetic sculptures incorporate movement as an integral element, creating dynamic visual experiences that captivate and mesmerise. Robotic performances showcase the capabilities of robots as performers or collaborators, pushing the boundaries of traditional performance art. Robotic theatre reimagines the stage by integrating robots as performers, challenging traditional notions of human-centred performance. Animatronics, a specialised form of creative robotics, focuses on creating lifelike robotic characters and creatures for entertainment and artistic purposes. The artistic landscape of creative robotics extends far beyond these examples, with boundless potential for further exploration and innovation.  
*(Samani, 2023, p137)*

AI and ML techniques have found their way into robotic art, enabling robots to learn, adapt, and create in collaboration with human artists. These techniques allow robots to analyse and interpret sensory data, generate novel artistic content, and even learn from human artists or previous artistic experiences.

Artists can train ML models to recognise and classify visual or auditory patterns, allowing robots to respond creatively to their surroundings. Neural networks and deep learning algorithms can generate new artworks based on learned styles or patterns, adding a unique twist to the artistic process.  
*(Ibid, p155)*

AI and machine learning techniques can enhance the expressive capabilities of robotic art by enabling robots to learn and respond to their environment and human interactions. Through sensor inputs and data analysis, robots can adapt their behaviours and artistic expressions in real-time, creating dynamic and interactive experiences.

For example, robots can learn to interpret and respond to human gestures, emotions, or speech, allowing for personalised and context-aware artistic interactions. This integration of AI and machine learning with robotics expands the boundaries of creative expression, fostering a deeper connection between the artwork, the robot, and the observer.  
*(Ibid, p158)*
